Method notes — read before comparing two rows

Return on invested capital

ROIC = EBIT × (1 − effective tax rate) / (book equity + net debt); when a year's effective rate is outside 0–60% (tax credit, one-off, loss) the median of the company's normal years is used, or N/D. Goodwill is included, because it sits inside book equity, so companies built by acquisition show lower returns. R&D is expensed as filed and not capitalised, so R&D-heavy companies show higher returns than an economic measure would. Operating leases are excluded from debt; finance leases are included.

Net debt

Long- and short-term debt + finance leases − cash − short-term investments. Short-term investments are deducted only when reported for that year — under a US GAAP tag, a company-specific XBRL element (NVIDIA since 2026, Deere's long-term debt since 2022), or the Yahoo line for secondary-source companies; otherwise they are not deducted and the audit trail says so. Every week each component's tag is audited for migrations. That can only overstate net debt, never understate it.

ROIC above WACC, year by year

Each past year is compared with the WACC shown, not with that year's WACC: historical WACCs are not reconstructed. When rates were lower, past spreads were wider than shown. A year is marked n/m when invested capital was below the published share of total assets: ROIC is then the artefact of a near-zero denominator.

Secondary source

Companies with no free machine-readable filing — Swiss issuers (no ESEF, no SEC filing), Fanuc (EDINET requires a key), Saudi Aramco, China Northern Rare Earth, Kazatomprom, and European reports not indexed on filings.xbrl.org (Safran, Rheinmetall) or incomplete there (Leonardo, Coats) — use Yahoo Finance annual statements: four to five years, standardised by the aggregator. They are drawn in grey and line definitions may differ from the filing. They are tested every week for accounting identities (assets = liabilities + equity, gross profit, EPS × shares), and Leonardo and Coats are also compared with their ESEF report.

IFRS and US GAAP are not on the same basis

Under IAS 38, IFRS filers capitalise development costs that meet set criteria, while US GAAP expenses research and development. Ferrari, TSMC, Hermès, Leonardo and Coats report under IFRS: their EBIT and invested capital are not directly comparable with Apple's or Microsoft's.

EBIT used for the no-growth value (EPV)

Last fiscal year's operating income, with one-off items removed (impairments, restructuring). There is no averaging over the business cycle, so for cyclical companies such as Caterpillar, Deere or ExxonMobil the EPV reflects where the cycle stood that year.

Why the vertical lines sit at the risk-free rate

A growth rate that lasts forever cannot exceed the long-run nominal growth of the economy whose currency discounts the cash flows. The risk-free rate is the usual market proxy for it (Damodaran's convention), and the engine caps perpetual growth at min(risk-free, cost of equity − 2 pts). Each line therefore shows the same cap the valuation uses, in the currency of the accounts. Nominal GDP growth would be the economic constraint itself; it is not shown yet. TSMC: there is no free 10-year yield in Taiwan dollars, so the US 10-year is used and flagged.

Move the growth and discount-rate sliders to see what the price requires. Every underlined number opens its source: formula, inputs, XBRL tag and the filing it comes from.

06

Method & limits

How it is computed, and what it cannot tell you

Where the numbers come from

  • US companies: annual 10-K, foreign issuers: 20-F, both read directly from SEC EDGAR XBRL.
  • European companies: ESEF annual reports from filings.xbrl.org; when a report is missing or incomplete, and for Swiss, Japanese, Saudi, Chinese and Kazakh issuers, Yahoo Finance statements (secondary source, in grey).
  • Prices and FX: daily closes, free sources. Figures are in the currency of the accounts and never converted in a calculation; absolute levels also show the header currency underneath, with the rate, its date and its source. Ten-year series are never converted.
  • Every week filed figures are cross-checked against a second source, every company's accounts are tested for accounting identities, and the gaps are published (click the quality badge).

What is computed

  • Implied growth: the perpetual growth g that solves EV = FCF × (1+g) / (WACC − g). FCF is adjusted for stock-based pay; when last year's FCF is negative, the median of the last five years is used if positive, and flagged.
  • EPV: value with zero growth, EBIT × (1 − t) / WACC − net debt.
  • WACC: risk-free rate of the currency of the accounts (US Treasury, Bund…) + β × equity risk premium (Damodaran's monthly implied ERP, date shown in every audit trail; one mature-market premium for every currency), β measured on five years of weekly returns in USD against the MSCI World and Blume-adjusted. Detailed notes sit under the coverage table.
  • Sustainable growth: ROIC × reinvestment rate, from the accounts only.
  • Lab: daily returns rebalanced to target weights; VaR and CVaR are historical (95 %, one day); Sharpe and Sortino use the 10-year yield of the measuring currency.
  • Best Sharpe / lowest volatility: long-only, at most 20% per company, fitted on the companies you keep — with an out-of-sample check, because fitted weights always flatter the past.
  • Range of outcomes: 2,000 paths drawn in one-month blocks from the allocation's own last five years (block bootstrap). A spread, not a forecast.

Limits

  • Missing data stays N/D: nothing is imputed. A ratio whose formula stops measuring what it claims is shown as n/m, with the reason.
  • A perpetuity formula is very sensitive near WACC = g; read the slope, not one point.
  • Past crises do not repeat in the same way. Holdings not listed at the time are excluded.
  • Rivals are an editorial pairing for one common market, not a full peer group.
  • Freshness: a badge turns amber when revenue growth since the last annual accounts exceeds 15% a year pro rata (four-quarter figures from 10-Qs where filed).
